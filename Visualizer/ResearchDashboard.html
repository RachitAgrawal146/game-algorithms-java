Central Finding

Pruning works when it's cheaper than the search it eliminates. In Connect4, alpha-beta pruning adds near-zero overhead (just two integer comparisons) while eliminating 90% of an exponential tree — a massive net win. In N-Queens, forward checking eliminates 58% of nodes but adds domain copying and propagation at every surviving node — the per-node overhead exceeds the per-node savings at large N. In Mastermind, constraint elimination is expensive per step but operates on a small, finite candidate set — the overhead is justified because the search space is bounded.

The efficiency of pruning depends not on how many branches it cuts, but on the ratio of pruning cost to search cost. This ratio is determined by the problem's constraint structure, not by the algorithm alone.

Implication 1

When evaluating algorithms, node count alone is misleading. FC's 58% node reduction in N-Queens masks a 3x wall-clock slowdown. Practical performance requires measuring both nodes and cost-per-node.

Implication 2

Stochastic methods escape the pruning-cost tradeoff entirely. Min-Conflicts doesn't prune — it doesn't even search. By reformulating CSPs as optimisation, it sidesteps the overhead question and operates in O(n) average time.
